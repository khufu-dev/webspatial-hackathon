About the model, controls & visual references

On screen, drag to orbit and scroll or pinch to zoom. Select a planet or the Sun in the view or the navigation bar. In VR, point with a controller or hand and select a body; use the floating Overview, Pause / play, Next body, and Exit VR controls. Your head movement stays independent of the model.

Positions use JPL’s approximate Keplerian elements with secular rates, evaluated by a double-precision WebAssembly solver. Dates are limited to 1800–2050. Earth uses the Earth–Moon barycenter, and UTC approximates dynamical time. This is an educational model, not a navigation ephemeris. Moons and gravitational perturbations are not simulated.

Explore scale compresses orbital distances and enlarges planets. True scale uses one linear scale for distances and radii; planets are tiny in the overview, so use the navigation bar to visit them. Rotation rates and axial tilts are modeled; prime meridians and pole directions are illustrative. Sunlight direction is geometric, with exposure balanced to keep distant planets visible.

Inspired by Thomas Ricouard’s Astra demonstration. Planetary maps are from Solar System Scope, based on NASA imagery. The celestial panorama is ESO/S. Brunier’s eso0932a, displayed with reduced exposure and an illustrative orientation. The 2K planetary maps and 4K panorama are stored locally; cloud opacity, sunlight, and atmospheric scattering are applied during rendering.
